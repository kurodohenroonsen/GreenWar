01 — Historical Genesis

The Genesis of Institutionalized Extraction

Following World War II and the subsequent economic expansion, nations sought to establish the doctrine of the three pillars.

They acted out of fear, on the one hand, of a depletion of consumer goods caused by exhausted production ecosystems, and on the other, of an irreversible global degradation of atmospheric quality caused by overproduction pollution.

Today, this framework perpetuates ecological depletion and the impoverishment of non-westernized regions under the guise of legal international treaties.

02 — The Three Pillars

Three Pillars, Each with a Single Goal

Each pillar pursues its own institutional survival. None has the objective of leaving the planet stronger than it found it.

Pillar 01

The Economic Pillar

Composed of corporations, it manufactures and supplies unlimited goods and services using the cheapest available labor to sell through any channel, to anyone, anywhere, anytime.

Profitability is the sole objective. Les entreprises ne fabriquent des produits écologiques que si cette stratégie leur permet d'être plus concurrentielles.

Pillar 02

The Associative Pillar

Composed of non-profit entities and foundations offering social services to fulfill their institutional objectives.

Their survival and renown are the sole objectives. Ces organismes ponctionnent légalement des subventions d'entreprises polluantes pour reconstruire, de manière non rentable, un petit bout de tissu déchiré.

Pillar 03

The Environmental Pillar

Composed of state governments, it regulates products and services. Justifying budget expenditures and securing political recognition are its sole objectives.

Regulations grant tax relief to clean corporations while shifting the tax burden onto Consumer-Citizens through domestic levies.

03 — Historical Assessment

The Three Quests: Money, Power, and Glory

Nations govern the world through the three classic branches of power — legislative, executive, and judicial — which enable the three pillars to legitimately pursue conquests that ravage our biosphere.

These three pillars have replaced medieval warfare: they no longer set nation against nation, but the rich against the poor within every society. Confronted with planetary boundaries, this model has reached its exhaustion point.
